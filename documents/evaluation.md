*   **Reward Mapping:** The reward function was synthesized from the `@safety` and `@progress` annotations within the formal model.

**Results:** The agent achieved a high mean reward (\(\approx 1.97 \times 10^5\)) and maximum episode lengths. However, state evolution plots (Fig. X) revealed that the agent learned to maintain a constant velocity of \(v=0\).

While the agent was **formally safe**, it lacked **liveness**. This confirmed that a direct mapping of safety logic is insufficient for functional control and necessitated the introduction of progress-oriented annotations.

### 5.3 Test 2: System Validation via Forced Dynamics
In the second stage of evaluation, we conducted a diagnostic stress test to verify the integration of the ODE solver and the safety monitoring system. To ensure the environment was functioning correctly beyond the "safe-lock" (static) state, we manually restricted the agent’s action space, forcing it to exclusively select the acceleration branch (\(a := A\)) of the `controlFunc`.

**Results:** As expected, the agent transitioned from a static state to a linearly increasing velocity profile. The simulation successfully captured the physical movement of the robot; however, the episodes were consistently truncated after approximately 10 steps. 

**Analysis:** This test provided critical validation for two components of our framework:
1.  **Physics Integration:** The smooth, linear increase in velocity and the resulting change in coordinates confirmed that the transpiler correctly mapped the symbolic ODEs to the numerical solver.
2.  **Safety Monitor Accuracy:** The rapid truncation of episodes demonstrated that the safety monitor was functioning as intended. As the agent was forced to accelerate, its velocity \(v\) increased, causing the required stopping distance—defined by the quadratic relationship \(\text{stopDist}(v) = \frac{v^2}{2b}\)—to expand. The environment correctly identified the exact moment the agent's "safety buffer" exceeded the available separation distance, triggering a safety violation.

**Conclusion:** While the agent "failed" the task, the experiment was a success for the system architecture. It proved that the environment does not merely simulate movement, but actively enforces the formal boundaries of the \(\text{d}\mathcal{L}\) model in real-time. This confirmed that the synthesized environment was ready for complex training where the agent must learn to balance acceleration with the braking branch (\(a := -b\)).
